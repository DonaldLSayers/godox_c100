# Godox C100 — palettes v2 (more colours, brightness-weighted matching)

_2026-10-14. Status: **simulated only, not yet on the camera.** Follows
[2026-10-13-image-processing.md](2026-10-13-image-processing.md)._

## Why

The first daylight photos with the v1 palettes looked over-compressed: big
areas collapse into a single colour and you can't make out much. Three causes:

1. **Most v1 palettes really had 5 colours.** In 5 of the 7 palettes the 6th
   entry was a padded copy of the 5th.
2. **Big brightness gaps.** e.g. riso6 jumps from navy (Y=30) to the next
   colour at Y=115, so every shadow between them becomes flat navy.
3. **Hue counted as much as brightness** in the nearest-colour search, while
   brightness is what makes a photo readable.

## What changes

| Setting | v1 (on camera now) | v2 (this doc) |
|---|---|---|
| Palettes | 7 | 13 (see below) |
| Colours per palette | 6 slots (often 5 distinct) | **at least 10** (small palettes topped up to 10; big ones 16–55) |
| Match distance | `ΔY + ΔU + ΔV` (absolute) | **`2·ΔY + ΔU + ΔV`** |
| Dither | 4×4 Bayer on block luma, ±32 | unchanged |
| Block | 2×2 (one 4:2:0 chroma pair) | unchanged |
| `CONTRAST` / `BRIGHT` | 100 / 0 | unchanged |
| Output size | 640 wide | unchanged |

### The palette set

- **Kept from v1:** riso6, vibrant_nature, refreshing.
- **Dropped:** vibrant_summer, fiesta, bold_hues, dark_sunset.
- **Added, small (5 colours, topped up to 10):** mid_century, harbour, earthy.
  (seventies was tried and dropped.)
- **Added, pixel-art palettes (canonical Lospec values):** pixnote24 (24),
  pico8 (16), db16 (16), db32 (32), endesga32 (32), sweetie16 (16), nes (55).

The pixel-art palettes' hex values were taken from screenshots. Sampled after
applying the screenshot's colour profile, they match the published values to
±1, so the published values are used. Two exceptions: Pixnote 24's pastels
are as sampled, and the screenshot showed only the first 32 NES colours. The
rest of the NES list is the standard 2C02 palette from memory (55 here, while
the source card says 53), so a few of its tail colours may differ.

### Topping small palettes up to 10: "overprint" mixes

Each small palette is topped up to 10 with **midpoints of two base colours**,
like two riso inks printed over each other (navy + teal → dark teal, navy +
pink → plum), so the new colours stay in the palette's family. They are picked
greedily: each step adds the mix that is farthest (Manhattan, Y+U+V) from
every colour already in the set, so the biggest gaps get filled first. Mixes
that come out greyer than their less-saturated parent are skipped (grey mixes,
e.g. navy + cream, made everything muddy in tests).

### Big palettes, and cutting them down to 10

Matching against 16–55 colours costs 2–5× the work of 10 (see Firmware
impact). So each big palette also has a **10-colour cut**, chosen on real
photos by [`tools/palette_pick.py`](../tools/palette_pick.py): starting from
the full palette, it keeps dropping the colour whose loss hurts the match
least on 35 stock-firmware photos. (A first try that just picked the 10 most
different colours gave garish, extreme results.) The cut is stored in `PICK`
in `palettes.py`.

**Result: the cuts lose what makes these palettes nice.** The training photos
are mostly indoor and the C100's raw colours are washed out, so the cuts keep
greys and browns and drop greens and saturated colours (db32's 10 are almost
all greys; grass turns brown). The full palettes look far better, close to
photographic. So the full palettes are the ones worth getting onto the camera
(see the lookup-table idea below).

All palette data lives in [`tools/palettes.py`](../tools/palettes.py):

```
python3 tools/palettes.py        # every palette at 10 (big ones: their cut)
python3 tools/palettes.py full   # big palettes whole
python3 tools/palettes.py asm    # the 10-colour .byte table for photo_cave.s
```

### Observations

- The small palettes stay strongly stylised. riso6 and refreshing read best:
  a real dark colour plus a spread of mid-tones.
- **mid_century and harbour have no dark colour** (darkest Y = 81 and 65),
  and a mix can't be darker than its darkest parent. Shadows turn into one
  flat dark teal (mid_century) or plum (harbour). If that's unwanted, give
  them a dark base colour (e.g. a near-black navy or brown).
- With brightness counting double, hue matters less: green grass can go pink
  or red in riso6, since riso6 has no green. That's part of the palette's
  character, and the shapes read much better than in v1.
- The full pixel-art palettes look like a real photo in a retro palette: much
  more recognisable, but softer and less "poster-like" than the small ones.
  Palettes with several greys (pixnote24, nes, db32) drift towards grey,
  because the camera's raw colours are pale.
- `sim_palette.py --sat` tries a chroma boost before matching. 160% makes
  db32 and sweetie16 grass a bit greener, but the effect is small. It's not in
  the v2 settings.

## Firmware impact (for the next step)

- **Small palettes at 10 colours** (the plan before the big palettes were
  added). The table is 3 B × 10 per palette, so it no longer fits the 128 B
  slot at `0xbb0c0` and has to move. The matcher grows from 6 to 10 unrolled
  comparisons (+224 B; about 330 B is free between the hook's literal pool
  and `BAYER`), about 40% more work per block. The `2·ΔY` weight costs
  nothing (`mov lr, r6, lsl #1` instead of `mov lr, r6`).
- **Big palettes whole:** a straight search costs 2–5× the 10-colour work and
  would very likely miss the per-strip deadline at 640 wide. That shows up as
  the lower part of the photo turning to noise; recoverable by re-flashing.
- **Lookup table (the way to get big palettes on the camera).** Once per photo
  (in `idx_stub`, outside the interrupt), fill a table that maps a quantised
  colour (e.g. Y/8 × U/16 × V/16 = 32×16×16 = 8 KB) straight to its nearest
  palette colour. The per-block cost then becomes a single table read, cheaper
  than today's 6 comparisons and the same for any palette size. Cost: 8 KB of
  RAM (more than the ~2 KB cave, so it needs a buffer from the firmware's
  `malloc`, `thunk_FUN_0005ca54`) and a few ms per photo to fill it. The
  quantisation steps should be checked in the simulator first.
- **Timing fallbacks:** brightness weight only (6 colours, no extra cost),
  or fewer colours.
