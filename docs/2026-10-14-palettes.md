# Godox C100 — palettes v2 (10–32 colours, brightness-weighted matching)

_2026-10-14. Status: **on the camera and working** (photos IMG_0199–0209); the
palette tag is built and emulator-verified, not yet flashed. Follows
[2026-10-13-image-processing.md](2026-10-13-image-processing.md)._

## Why

The first daylight photos with the v1 palettes looked over-compressed: big
areas collapse into a single colour and you can't make out much. Three causes:

1. **Most v1 palettes really had 5 colours.** In 5 of the 7 palettes the 6th
   entry was a padded copy of the 5th.
2. **Big brightness gaps.** e.g. riso6 jumps from navy (Y=30) to the next
   colour at Y=115, so every shadow between them becomes flat navy.
3. **Hue counted as much as brightness** in the nearest-colour search, while
   brightness is what makes a photo readable.

## What changes

| Setting | v1 (on camera now) | v2 (this doc) |
|---|---|---|
| Palettes | 7 | 8 (see below) |
| Colours per palette | 6 slots (often 5 distinct) | **10 to 32** |
| Match distance | `ΔY + ΔU + ΔV` (absolute) | **`2·ΔY + ΔU + ΔV`** |
| Colour search | 6 unrolled comparisons per block | **lookup table**, filled once per photo (see Firmware) |
| Palette per photo | next in the cycle (random start per boot) | **random** each photo, never the same twice in a row |
| Dither | 4×4 Bayer on block luma, ±32 | unchanged |
| Block | 2×2 (one 4:2:0 chroma pair) | unchanged |
| `CONTRAST` / `BRIGHT` | 100 / 0 | unchanged |
| Output size | 640 wide | unchanged |

### Small palettes: topped up to 10 with "overprint" mixes

Each 5–6 colour palette is topped up to 10 with **midpoints of two base
colours**, like two riso inks printed over each other (navy + teal → dark
teal, navy + pink → plum), so the new colours stay in the palette's family.
They are picked greedily: each step adds the mix that is farthest (Manhattan,
Y+U+V) from every colour already in the set, so the biggest gaps get filled
first. Mixes that come out greyer than their less-saturated parent are skipped
(grey mixes, e.g. navy + cream, made everything muddy in tests).

### db16: topped up to 24

db16 came out muted and slightly purple at 16. It gets 8 extra colours
(`SIZE` in `palettes.py`), all mixes of two db16 colours, even though that
departs from the original palette. Grass comes out greener and the purple
cast is lighter.

### mid_century: two dark colours added

mid_century's darkest colour was `#126D68` (Y = 81), and a mix can't be darker
than its darkest parent, so every shadow came out as one flat teal. It now has
two extra base colours: a dark teal `#093634` and a dark navy `#1A1B3A`
(riso6's navy). The navy takes the deepest shadows, the dark teal the ones just
above, and the mixes now include a rust brown. Comparison (original / + dark
teal / + dark navy / + both):
[previews/palettes_v2/mid_century_dark.png](../previews/palettes_v2/mid_century_dark.png).

An experiment with **shades and tints** (a base colour mixed half with black
or white, to top up earthy to 16) was dropped together with earthy. It also
showed that the order of the top-up pools matters: putting shades/tints
before the greying mixes changed harbour's 10th colour from the dark plum
`#584056` to a light mint, and harbour's shadows turned harsh red and blue.

### NES: thinned from 55 to 32

The full NES list has many near-duplicates (e.g. `FCFCFC`/`F8F8F8`,
`7C7C7C`/`787878`, several almost-equal blues and greens). `thin()` in
`palettes.py` repeatedly takes the closest pair and drops whichever of the two
is closer to the rest of the set, until 32 are left. One of each grey
(`000000`, `7C7C7C`, `BCBCBC`, `FCFCFC`) is protected, because photos lean on
greys; without that, the light grey went first. Dropped:

`F8F8F8` `0078F8` `B8B8F8` `6888FC` `0000BC` `D8B8F8` `6844FC` `F8A4C0`
`F0D0B0` `A81000` `FCE0A8` `FCA044` `E45C10` `881400` `F8D878` `00B800`
`006800` `B8F8D8` `00A844` `005800` `00E8D8` `F8D8F8` `787878`

Caveat: the source screenshot showed only the first 32 NES colours; the rest
of `NES_FULL` is the standard 2C02 list from memory (55 here, while the source
says 53), so a few of those may differ. Note that both skin tones (`F0D0B0`,
`FCE0A8`) were thinned out.

### Observations

- The small palettes stay strongly stylised. riso6 and refreshing read best:
  a real dark colour plus a spread of mid-tones.
- harbour has no really dark colour (darkest Y = 65); its shadows come out
  as a flat dark plum (`#584056`, a mix of its red and blue). mid_century
  had the same problem until it got its dark teal and navy.
- With brightness counting double, hue matters less: green grass can go pink
  or red in riso6, since riso6 has no green.
- db16 at 24 is a bit greener and less purple than at 16.
- The big palettes (db16, endesga32, sweetie16, nes) look like a real photo in
  a retro palette: much more recognisable, softer than the small ones.
- Cutting the big palettes to 10 was tried and rejected. Picking the most
  different colours looked garish; picking the 10 that best reproduce 35
  stock photos kept mostly greys and browns (grass turned brown). They need
  their full colour set.
- `sim_palette.py --sat` tries a chroma boost before matching. 160% makes
  grass a bit greener in the big palettes, but the effect is small. It's not
  in the v2 settings.

## Firmware: lookup-table matching

Searching 32 colours per block is about 5× v1's work and would very likely
miss the per-strip deadline at 640 wide (the lower part of the photo turns to
noise). Instead the colour search moves out of the interrupt into a lookup
table that is filled once per photo.

### Choosing the table size

Bin size, simulated with `sim_palette.py --lut YS CS` (share of blocks that
come out different from the exact search, IMG_0184 / IMG_0186; db16 was
measured at 16 colours):

| Steps (Y, U/V) | Entries | Size | riso6 | db16 | endesga32 | nes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8, 16 | 33×17×17 | ~9.5 KB | 12% / 8% | 14% / 14% | 23% / 17% | 16% / 15% |
| 8, 8 | 33×33×33 | ~36 KB | 8% / 6% | 10% / 11% | 16% / 13% | 8% / 11% |

Bins must be **centred on multiples of the step** (index = `(x + step/2) >>
shift`). The first try used plain `x >> shift`, which puts neutral grey
(U=V=128) on a bin edge and gave every grey a pink cast. With centred bins,
the 36 KB table is visually indistinguishable from the exact search; the
9.5 KB one shows slight colour shifts on large flat areas. **Chosen: 36 KB**
(33×33×33 = 35,937 entries). The previews use the same quantisation
(`LUT` in `palettes.py`), so they show exactly what the camera produces.

### Implementation ([`tools/patches/photo_cave.s`](../tools/patches/photo_cave.s))

**Per photo — `idx_stub` → `lut_build`** (hooks `FUN_000187c4`, the capture
handler; it runs in the camera task, before the capture starts):

1. **Random palette per photo.** Index = (FreeRTOS tick count & 0xff) mod
   `NPAL`. The tick count (`xTickCount` at `0x126b80`, found via
   `xTaskIncrementTick` `FUN_0005df2c` and `xTaskGetTickCount` `FUN_0005dee4`)
   counts since boot, so its low bits at the moment of the shutter press are
   effectively random. If it equals the previous photo's palette, the next one
   is used, so the same palette never comes twice in a row. This replaces v1's
   cycling and the per-boot seed from sensor noise.
2. **Table buffer**, allocated once with `pvPortMalloc(35937, 64)` (thunk at
   `0x5374c` → `FUN_0005ca54`) and kept. If the allocation fails, the photo is
   left unprocessed and the next photo tries again.
3. **Fill:** for every bin centre (Y, U, V in 0, 8, …, 256) find the nearest
   palette colour (`2·|dY| + |dU| + |dV|`, first one wins on a tie, as in the
   simulator) and store its byte offset (3 × index) in the table. The last
   bin's centre is 256, not 255, which adds the same amount to every distance,
   so the winner is the same.
4. Set `CUR_PAL` (the palette's colours) last; while it is 0 the hook leaves
   strips alone.

**Per strip — `hook`** (interrupt, as before): block luma average, CONTRAST /
BRIGHT, Bayer, read U, V, then `((yq·33 + uq)·33 + vq)` with
`q = (x + 4) >> 3` (multiplying by 33 is `x + (x << 5)`), one table read, and
the palette colour written to the block. That's **66 instructions per block**,
against 147 for v1's 6-colour search (both counted in the emulator, v1 on the
v008 backup), so 640 wide has more headroom than before.

**Palette data:** generated by `palettes.py asm` into
`build/obj/palettes.inc` (`.equ NPAL` plus a `PALETTE_DATA` macro: per palette
a count byte, then the Y, U, V triplets), which `build.sh` creates and
`photo_cave.s` includes. 8 palettes, 144 colours, 440 B.

**Cave layout** (all still inside dead `FUN_000bab6c`, 0xbab6c–0xbb340; as
of the palette-tag build):

| Address | Contents |
|---|---|
| `0xbab6c` | `hook` (fixed: `hook_jump.s` branches here) |
| `0xbad44` | tag drawing (end of `hook`) |
| `0xbae80` | `lut_build` |
| `0xbafcc` | `BAYER`, then `VARS` (`LUT_PTR`, `CUR_PAL`, `PAL_IDX`, `STRIP_N`) |
| `0xbafec` | `PALETTES`, then `TAGS` |
| `0xbb234` | aspect-ratio stubs `photo43`, `stub169`, `stub32`, `stub11` |
| `0xbb2f4` | `idx_stub`: `b lut_build` (ends `0xbb2f8`) |

Only `hook` has a fixed address. `build.sh` reads the others from the object
file (`llvm-nm`) and patches the resolution jump table and `cyc_jump.s` with
them, so the layout can change freely.

Labels in literal pools are written as `label - base` so they assemble to
absolute constants instead of relocations (the image is loaded at 0).

### Palette tag

Every photo gets its palette's two-letter tag in the top right corner, so you
can tell which palette it got:

| riso6 | refreshing | mid_century | harbour | db16 | endesga32 | sweetie16 | nes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R6 | RF | MC | HB | DB | EG | S6 | NS |

- **Font:** 3×5 pixels per letter (`FONT` in `palettes.py`), one pixel = 2×2
  blocks = 4×4 image pixels. With a 1-pixel gap and a 1-pixel outline the tag
  is 9×7 font pixels = 36×28 image pixels, 8 px from the top and right edges.
- **Colours:** letters in the palette's lightest colour (by Y), outline (the
  8 neighbours of every letter pixel) in its darkest, so the tag stays within
  the palette. Elsewhere the photo shows through.
- **Data:** `palettes.py asm` precomputes each palette's 9×7 grid as 2-bit
  cells (16 B) plus the dark and light colour offsets: 18 B per palette.
- **Firmware:** `lut_build` resets a strip counter (`STRIP_N`); after the hook
  has processed a strip, it checks the counter and on a photo's first strip it
  overwrites the tag's blocks (2×2 blocks per font pixel, always in the right
  half, so the chroma sits in the odd row), before the cache clean.
- The previews and `sim_palette.render(..., tag=...)` draw the same tag.

This assumes the scaler delivers a photo's top strip first. That's how the
strips are laid out, but it's only confirmed once a tagged photo comes off the
camera.

### Verification ([`tools/emu_cave.py`](../tools/emu_cave.py))

The built firmware is run in an ARM emulator (Unicorn), with the cache
maintenance calls and `pvPortMalloc` stubbed. It runs `idx_stub` for a series
of tick values, then feeds the hook a 640×64 strip of a real photo in the
camera's 4:2:0 layout. Results:

- every 2×2 block is exactly the palette colour the simulator picks, for all
  8 palettes, on IMG_0184 and IMG_0186 (0.00% difference), including the
  palette tag on a photo's first strip and no tag on the second;
- the palette follows the tick (300 → 300 & 0xff = 44 → 44 mod 8 = 4), and a
  repeat is bumped (tick 7 twice → palettes 7, then 0);
- `pvPortMalloc` is called once, on the first photo;
- with a failing allocation the strip comes back byte-for-byte untouched.

```
uv run --with unicorn,numpy,pillow tools/emu_cave.py images/IMG_0184.jpg
```

### Timing

- Strip hook: 45% of v1's per-block work (66 vs 147 instructions).
- Table fill: 15.8M instructions for a 24-colour palette, 21M for 32 (counted
  in the emulator), so roughly 50–150 ms added before each capture,
  depending on the CPU clock. Untested on the device: if the shutter feels
  slow, the fill can be made cheaper (e.g. skip colours that can't win for a
  given Y).
